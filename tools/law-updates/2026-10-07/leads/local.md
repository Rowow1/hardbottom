# Local ordinances track: legal review, 7 Oct 2026

Scope: counties, cities, towns and special districts, Florida coast. Posture: every local rule is treated as live; no preemption argument is made. Method: Municode's public JSON API (`api.municode.com/search?clientId=..&contentTypeId=CODES&mode=CLIENTMODE&searchText=..&fragmentSize=..`, and `CodesContent?jobId=..&nodeId=..&productId=..`) and American Legal pages, read through WebFetch. WebFetch's summariser returns text in quoted pieces, so every quote below is "API transcription, near-verbatim" until re-read in a browser at the URL given. Library deep link for any Municode NodeId: `https://library.municode.com/fl/<client-slug>/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=<NodeId>`.

What changed in method since 30 Sep: that sweep searched spear, scuba, snorkel, diving and harpoon. This pass added "fishing", "projectile", "crab traps", "hook and line", "inlet", "jetty" and "canal". Those terms found rules that ban spearing without naming it: canal gear acts (hook and line only), park-water fishing bans, citywide air/spring weapon bans, and swim bans at piers, jetties and inlets. Municode client ids were resolved from `browser-items.json` and from `api.municode.com/Clients/name?clientName=<name>&stateAbbr=<fl|FL>` (works intermittently; spaces, not underscores).

One statutory fact for the main session, not an argument: Fla. Stat. § 379.2412 reserves "the power to regulate the taking or possession of saltwater fish" to the state, and says it does not stop a local government banning saltwater fishing on real property it owns (paraphrase; Online Sunshine). Martin County (July 2026) and Belleair Shore cite it. Several hits below are special acts (Laws of Fla.), not ordinances. Not in the registry today.
