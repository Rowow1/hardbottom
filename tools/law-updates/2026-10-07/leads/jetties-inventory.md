# Florida jetty inventory for r. 68B-20.003(2)(d)

Working file, 7 Oct 2026. Built to support tracing the 100 ft jetty buffer statewide. Rows are added
coast by coast; the summary sections at the end are written last.

Conventions. Coordinates are `[lat, lon]`, 4 decimals, and every coordinate here is APPROXIMATE
unless marked otherwise: they come from FWC piers.json points or are left blank for the main session
to take from OpenStreetMap and imagery. Lengths are as documented, with the source; "no length found"
means none was located in the time spent, not that none exists. Non-primary sources are marked LEAD.

Key sources used throughout:
- CP4 ch. 8 (Savannah River to St. Johns River): https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C08_WEB.pdf
- CP4 ch. 9 (St. Johns River): https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C09_WEB.pdf
- CP4 ch. 10 (St. Johns River to Miami): https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C10_WEB.pdf

## The long-jetty exception: which Florida jetties could qualify

The exception reads: "except that spearfishing shall be allowed along the last 500 yards of any jetty that extends more than 1,500 yards from the shoreline." The threshold is 4,500 ft (1,372 m). The exempt stretch is the seaward 1,500 ft (457 m).

Finding: on documented lengths, only four Florida structures are long enough to be candidates, and all four are the 19th-century federal jetties at the St. Marys and St. Johns entrances. No other Florida jetty documented here comes within 400 ft of the threshold. None of the four is a clean case.

| Jetty | Documented structure length | Why it may still not qualify |
|---|---|---|
| St. Marys north (row 1) | 19,500 ft (FDEP, citing 1997 study) | Roots on Cumberland Island, Georgia. The Florida rule reaches only Florida waters; where the state line runs along the entrance needs checking before any part is treated as Florida. CP4 ch. 8: "both almost entirely submerged at mean high water". |
| St. Marys south (row 2) | 11,200 ft (same) | Same submergence. Structure length is not distance from the shoreline: the root sits well inside the inlet at Fort Clinch, and Amelia Island has accreted against the jetty since 1905. |
| St. Johns north (row 3) | 14,300 ft in 1937 (Sea Grant Report 38, citing the Chief of Engineers) | In 1881 the jetty was extended "westward to connect it with higher ground on Fort George Island", so much of its length runs along the inlet rather than out to sea. The 1934 cap ran "From the shoreline seaward ... to the full length of 3,550 ft", which, if the cap covered the whole seaward run, would put the 1934 seaward reach BELOW 4,500 ft. Against that, the FDEP Circumnavigational Paddling Trail text says the jetties "extend almost two miles into the Atlantic" (LEAD, non-engineering source: https://floridadep.gov/sites/default/files/CT_Segment%2025%20text%20June%202022_0.pdf). Huguenot Park and Ward's Bank have accreted since. Unresolved; needs imagery measurement. |
| St. Johns south (row 4) | 11,183 ft in 1937 (same) | Roots inside NAVSTA Mayport. The water around the south jetty tip is already closed 24/7 under 33 C.F.R. § 334.500, so the exception would open nothing there. |

Below the threshold, with sources: Ponce de Leon north 4,050 ft and south 4,018 ft (FDEP IMP); St. Lucie north 3,325 ft (FDEP 2023 IMP); Port Everglades submerged breakwaters "almost 0.7 mile" (about 3,700 ft, CP4, and wholly submerged anyway); Lake Worth about 2,000 ft; Fort Pierce north 1,600 to 1,800 ft; Port Canaveral north about 1,450 ft and south about 1,600 ft; everything else is under 1,100 ft or undocumented and visibly short.

Key West Northwest Channel jetties (rows 40, 41) are long but are not attached to any shoreline, so "extends ... from the shoreline" cannot be measured for them. Their documented exposed part is only "the outer part of the east jetty". Treat them as not qualifying.

How much is above water. CP4 is the only current source. St. Marys: "almost entirely submerged at mean high water" (ch. 8 ¶240). St. Johns: CP4 ch. 9 says nothing about submergence; the 1937 summary gives "crests 10 ft. above mlw" after the 1896 to 1934 raising work, but a 90-year-old crest figure says nothing reliable about settlement at the outer ends. Ponce north: "the inner end of the jetty is awash". St. Lucie north: "The inner part of the north jetty is in ruins." Hillsboro: "submerged at the entrance channel". Boynton: submerged rocks 15 ft past the south jetty tip. Port Everglades: a former jetty "covered 3 feet". Perdido (Alabama): over half the east jetty submerged 1 to 3 ft at low tide.

Recommendation for the map, following the project's conservative posture:
1. Draw no exemption anywhere until imagery shows a jetty that reaches more than 4,500 ft seaward of today's mean high water line, measured along the jetty from the point where it crosses that line. Use the shoreline position that gives the SHORTER distance where the shoreline is ambiguous, because a shorter distance closes more water.
2. The only jetty worth measuring is the St. Johns north jetty. Measure it on current imagery from the Huguenot Park beach line. If it clears 4,500 ft, mark the seaward 1,500 ft as an exception with a warning that the reading depends on where "the shoreline" is drawn and that no authority has interpreted it.
3. Do not draw an exemption for St. Marys: the north jetty is a Georgia-rooted structure, and the south jetty is mostly submerged, which leaves little buffer to exempt.
4. Do not draw an exemption for the St. Johns south jetty: the federal restricted area already closes that water.

## Interpretation of "jetty", "unsubmerged portion" and "shoreline"

Finding: no Florida court decision, Attorney General opinion or FWC rule interpretation of r. 68B-20.003(2)(d), or of any of its terms, was found. The rule's own definitions section does not define them. The only FWC statement is a plain-language paraphrase on its public spearing page.

What was searched:
- Florida case law through Legal Data Hunter (CourtListener and Caselaw Access Project, US-FL), keyword and semantic: "spearfishing within 100 feet of jetty", "spearfishing" with "jetty", "68B-20.003". No relevant hit. Confirmed negative within that corpus, which does not include unreported county-court citations.
- Florida AG opinions (myfloridalegal.com): the only spearfishing opinion found is AGO 56-281 (14 Sep 1956), on confiscating spearfishing gear under ch. 31133 (1955); it does not address jetties (https://www.myfloridalegal.com/histago/ago-56-281). The rule postdates it by three decades.
- FLRules notice 2610093 (1 Jan 1998 amendment) carries no explanation of (2)(d) (https://flrules.org/Gateway/View_notice.asp?id=2610093). The rule was first adopted 17 Jun 1985 by the Marine Fisheries Commission (formerly r. 46-20.003). The 1985 notice and rule-development record were not found online.

Definitions in the rule chapter. Fla. Admin. Code r. 68B-20.002 defines bow hunting, bow, crossbow, gigging, spearfishing and spearing only. It does not define "jetty", "shoreline", "unsubmerged", "groin" or "breakwater" (read via LII mirror, secondary: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-68B-20-002; confirm on FLRules before citing).

FWC's own paraphrase (agency web page, not law): "Within 100 feet of any part of a jetty that is above the surface of the sea" and "except for the last 500 yards of a jetty that extends more than 1,500 yards from the shoreline." (https://myfwc.com/fishing/saltwater/recreational/spearing/, read 7 Oct 2026). This is the closest thing to an FWC reading of "unsubmerged portion". It ties the test to the sea surface at the time, not to a tidal datum. On that wording, a section that covers and uncovers is inside the buffer whenever it shows. Conservative mapping: buffer every part that shows above water at LOW water, which matches what the project already did for the Lake Worth L-groin.

Other Florida law that defines the neighbouring terms (interpretive aids only; none of it governs ch. 68B-20):
- Fla. Admin. Code r. 62B-41.002 (DEP coastal construction): "Breakwater" is "a structure which has the effect of protecting shoreline areas, harbors, inlets" or "basins from the forces of currents and wave action"; "Groin" is "a structure built (usually perpendicular to the shoreline) to trap or alter alongshore movement of sediment" or "to retard erosion of the coastal system"; "Shoreline" is "the intersection of a specified plane of water with the shore or beach". It does not define jetty, and lists "groins, breakwaters, mound structures, jetties, weirs" as separate kinds of rigid coastal structure. Read via Justia mirror, secondary (https://regulations.justia.com/states/florida/62/62b/chapter-62b-41/section-62b-41-002); confirm on FLRules.
- Fla. Stat. § 161.061 lists "groins, jetties, moles, breakwaters, seawalls, revetments" as distinct structures (via public.law mirror, LEAD: https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._161.061). § 161.021 (2026) defines none of jetty, groin or breakwater.
- USACE Coastal Engineering Manual, EM 1110-2-1100, App. A glossary: JETTY "(1) (United States usage) On open seacoasts, a structure extending into a body of water, which is designed to prevent shoaling of a channel by littoral materials and to direct and confine the stream or tidal flow. Jetties are built at the mouths of rivers or tidal inlets to help deepen and stabilize a channel." GROIN: "Narrow, roughly shore-normal structure built to reduce longshore currents, and/or to trap and retain littoral material." (copy at https://luk.staff.ugm.ac.id/USACE/EM_1110-2-1100_App_A.pdf, LEAD for the copy; the manual itself is a USACE publication).

What this means for the map:
1. "Jetty" vs groin vs breakwater. Florida coastal law and USACE both treat these as distinct, which argues that a beach groin or a detached breakwater is not a "jetty". But the same structure is named differently by different agencies: the St. Augustine north jetty is "a terminal groin in the Corps of Engineers' design documents"; the John's Pass south structure is a "south jetty" in the FDEP IMP and "a terminal groin" in the FDEP SBMP; the Longboat Pass structure is a "jetty/terminal groin". The project's rule is to draw the reading that closes more water, so treat every shore-attached structure at an inlet (jetty, terminal groin, attached breakwater, spur) as a jetty. Leave beach groin fields away from inlets and fully detached breakwaters undrawn but listed, with a warning. Miami Beach's 29th to 33rd St "jetty area" is already closed by city code.
2. "Unsubmerged portion". FWC's paraphrase says "above the surface of the sea". Buffer every part visible at low water. Wholly submerged structures (Port Everglades breakwaters, St. Andrew inner submerged jetties, Massalina Bayou) carry no buffer under the rule as written, but they are hazards and should be marked rather than ignored.
3. "Shoreline" for the 1,500 yd test. Nothing defines it for this rule. Under r. 62B-41.002 a shoreline is tied to "a specified plane of water", which leaves the datum open. Use mean high water on current imagery and take the reading that gives the shorter distance.
4. Enforcement lead: a ScubaBoard thread reports that FWC officers, after checking with a supervisor, told a diver that spearing fish at the Fort Pickens rock jetties was "clearly a violation and a ticketable offense" (secondhand, undated, LEAD only: https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=5643996). It suggests FWC treats sound-side rock groins as jetties, but it is not evidence of an official position.
